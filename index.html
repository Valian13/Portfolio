The CNC Machine

in progress

A compact 3-axis mill I designed and built from scratch. This project used 2040 and 2060 aluminium extrusion, MGN9H linear rails, lead screw drive with anti-backlash nuts, four NEMA 17 steppers, and GRBL running on an Arduino Uno. Every bracket and carriage on it is a part I designed in SolidWorks and printed.

The hardest part isn't getting it to move, it's getting it to move to the same place twice with high precision. Most of that work has gone into anti-backlash nuts, bearing support and coupler alignment, plus a lot of re-printing brackets that were a fraction out.

On the electrical side I wired and configured the stepper drivers, calibrated steps/mm on each axis, and tuned acceleration and feed rates until the motion was smooth instead of skipping steps.

I designed the printed parts around what I could actually make. This means practicing DFMA via sensible tolerances, geometry that prints without supports, and an assembly order I wouldn't regret halfway through.
